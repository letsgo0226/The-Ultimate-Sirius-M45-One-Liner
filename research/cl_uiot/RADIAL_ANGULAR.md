# RACCO: conditional radial-angular convergence

Consider a physical orbit staying forever in min |x_i|² >= 3/5. Signs are constant because all multipliers are positive. UASO implies spread tends to one, hence width w=max |x_i|-min |x_i| tends to zero. Pauli covariance permits positive coordinates. Write m=mean |x_i|, g(m)=(5m³-3m⁵)/2. On [0,1]^3 each closed Bloch output has diagonal partial bounded in absolute value by 5/4 (the nonnegative term 5(b²+c²-b²c²) and 5a⁴ are each <=5), and each off-diagonal partial is <=5/2. A sharper bound for the comparison follows by replacing coordinates one at a time: sum |a_i-m| <= 4w/3, and each partial <=5/2. Thus |F_i(a)-g(m)|<=10w/3<=5w, so |m_next-g(m)|<=5w ->0.

Let l=liminf m_n and u=limsup m_n. Since g is continuous increasing on [0,1], the vanishing residual gives l=g(l), u=g(u). In the certified high region the only possibilities are alpha=sqrt(2/3) and 1. If l< u, select any t strictly between alpha and 1. Since g(t)>t, for sufficiently large n the residual is smaller than g(t)-t. Monotonicity gives m_n>=t implies m_next>t, contradicting l<t<u. Thus l=u, and the orbit converges to the corresponding signed critical point or deterministic vertex. Combined with ULCO, every orbit entering the central octahedron or eventually remaining in the high region converges. No proof yet that all physical orbits meet either condition; intermediate-region recurrence remains open. Exact rational tests check the finite-step error bound, not the asymptotic theorem.
