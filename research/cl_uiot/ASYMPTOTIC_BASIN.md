# ABCCO: relative completeness of open-basin certification

For any point converging to one of the five attracting fixed points, some finite iterate lies strictly inside the GIBCO direct certificate (vertex probability >9/10 or maximum Bloch amplitude squared <2/3). Each coordinate polynomial has nonnegative coefficients summing to at most 1024 and degree five, hence is 5120-Lipschitz in sup norm on [0,1]^4. For any box containing the point, endpoint evaluations and outward rounding bound the distance of each image endpoint from the true iterate by E_next <=5120 E+10^-digits. Clipping to [0,1] cannot increase distance. For fixed finite n the resulting error tends to zero as initial width and rounding unit tend to zero. Thus sufficiently narrow boxes containing the point are certified at that finite iterate, uniformly for all sufficiently large precisions.

Longest-side bisection reduces maximum width to at most 2^-floor(k/4) at depth k for the unit root. A complete binary tree through depth k has at most 2^(k+1)-1 nodes; the stage schedule processes all surviving nodes by this budget. For any point in an attracting basin and sufficiently large k, either an ancestor is already certified or its depth-k box satisfies the finite-iterate certificate. Consequently the union of certified leaves over all stages equals the union of the five attracting basins. This is relative completeness, not a proof that the complementary set consists only of stable manifolds of four saddles. Stages need not be nested. The current GIBCO precision cap of 100 digits restricts executable stages to k<=99; the unbounded-stage statement is an abstract theorem assuming arbitrary precision, not a claim about the current executable. No full cover run, merge or deployment.
