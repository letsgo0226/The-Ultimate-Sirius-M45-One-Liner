# Factorial convergence of Rosen Volterra iteration

On [-L,L], write V f=integral_0^u (u-v)K(v)f(v)dv and M>=sup|K|. Induction using the oriented integral gives |V^m f(u)| <= ||f|| M^m |u|^(2m)/(2m)!. Thus f=sum_{m>=0}(+/-V)^m 1 converges uniformly on any bounded interval for continuous K. It solves the integral equation by boundedness of V; uniqueness follows by iterating d=+/-Vd and taking m to infinity. This requires no contraction bound on the whole interval. For polynomial K use M=sum |k_i|L^i. With z=ML² and m=n+1, the tail after n iterations is bounded by z^m/(2m)! / (1-z/((2m+1)(2m+2))) whenever the denominator is positive. For every finite z such an n exists. The code requires the user to supply a sufficient n and otherwise rejects.

A positive lower bound for a polynomial approximation minus the certified error guarantees a nonsingular scale factor. A nonpositive bound is inconclusive, not proof of a zero. Solutions of the scalar ODE extend to every finite interval for globally continuous K, but Rosen coordinates may degenerate at zeros (e.g. cos u). No geodesic completeness, global Lorentzian chart or general Einstein convergence is claimed. No merge or deployment.
