# Local basin separation and finite orbit certificates

The closed Bloch map candidate is x'=x(5y²+5z²-x⁴-5y²z²)/4 and cyclic permutations. For a rigorous coefficient audit, compare the full polynomial to the enumerator (representative point tests alone do not prove equality). Given this identity, the sup norm m<=1/4 satisfies m'<= (10m³+6m⁵)/4 < m for m>0, giving a uniform attracting cube. Pauli vertex basins follow without this identity: multiplication of all five physical labels by a fixed Pauli is a logical Pauli, preserves syndrome and shifts output labels, so the identity error bound transfers to all vertices. Finite orbit certificates iterate exact rational probabilities and stop upon entering a sufficient basin; None means inconclusive. Rational bit sizes grow exponentially with depth; cap is a step limit, not a resource guarantee.

Local separation proof independent of the closed Bloch formula: near the critical point straighten the analytic stable graph by d=r-H(z). In these coordinates d'=d A(d,z), A(0,0)=5/3, and z'=G(d,z) with G(d,0)=0 because the symmetric line is invariant, and D_z G(0,0)=0. Choose a sufficiently small box |d|<=delta, ||z||<=eta with A between constants 1<a<b and ||G||<=||z||/2, including a slightly larger domain for the image. For d!=0 the sign persists until finite exit at |d| in [delta,b delta]. Along z=0 these two compact exit segments lie in the identity and uniform basins respectively, by the exact scalar dynamics. Both basins are open because their fixed points are locally attracting. Compactness gives a sufficiently small common transverse width around these segments contained in the respective basins. Shrink eta accordingly. Thus the two sides of the graph in a sufficiently small real neighborhood belong to the two different basins; points on the graph converge to the critical point. This is an existence proof of local separation, not an explicit radius for it or a global classification. The numeric uniform cube certificate depends on the polynomial identity audit.
