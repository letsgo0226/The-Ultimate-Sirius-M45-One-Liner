# Conditional-hazard reliability no-go

Let S_T mean no failure in the first T cycles, S_0 certain. Define h_t=P(first failure at cycle t | S_t) whenever P(S_t)>0. The chain rule gives P(S_T)=product_{t<T}(1-h_t); after survival reaches zero it remains zero, with subsequent hazards arbitrary for the product convention. If h_t>=delta>0 whenever defined, P(S_T)<=(1-delta)^T ->0. Independence is unnecessary. More generally if sum h_t diverges, survival tends to zero by 1-h<=exp(-h). If all h_t<1 and sum h_t converges, the infinite product is positive (eventually h_t<=1/2, and -log(1-h_t)<=2h_t). A single hazard of one forces zero survival. If sum h_t<=epsilon<1, union/first-failure bound gives survival >=1-epsilon. Finite fixed resources alone do NOT imply a positive hazard floor; noiseless systems or perfectly correctable restricted noise are counterexamples. Previous F^n is an upper certificate, not a physical lower hazard bound, so cannot establish delta. The code evaluates finite rational products and bounded threshold searches only. No physical permanence, hardware independence, merge or deployment.
