# Certified zeta reliability schedule

For integer s>=2 and m>=1, H_m=sum_{k=1}^m k^-s. Integral comparison yields H_m+(m+1)^(1-s)/(s-1) <= zeta(s) <= U=H_m+m^(1-s)/(s-1). Allocate e_t=epsilon/[U(t+1)^s] exactly as rational numbers. Their infinite sum is <=epsilon, possibly strictly less. If all conditional first-failure hazards meet these budgets, probability of any failure is <=epsilon. The finite-prefix planner stops on an uncertified cycle. For fixed 0<p<0.1, the bound (10p)^(2^n)/10 <= e_t requires sufficient depth O(log log(t+2)) asymptotically and data qubits O((log(t+2))^log2(5)). This improves the geometric schedule's sufficient asymptotic resource scaling, not a proof of optimality among summable schedules. Exact fractions grow quickly; finite tests do not establish physical resizing, noisy recovery, indefinite execution, or hardware independence. The zeta series here is real s>1; no use of zeta zeros or Riemann hypothesis. No merge or deployment.
